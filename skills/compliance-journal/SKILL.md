# Compliance journal

The journal is read by someone with authority over money. It is written to be checkable, dated and dull.

## The long-form period update

One entry per period, headed with its dates and a line linking it to the previous entry. Sections, in this order: outcomes received (each with the date and the stated reason, closed properly); interviews and recruiter calls (dated, with the role and the company or "confidential mandate via recruiter" where the client is confidential); applications made (a numbered list, company and role, date, one-line status); materials produced (a count of tailored documents); recruiter and search-firm engagement (names of firms, not of confidential clients); training and qualifications (passed, booked, in progress); profile, networking and speaking (posts, events, press); planned next steps. The planned steps in one entry are the first lines checked in the next.

## The job-entry list

Each entry: role title and company as the journal system wants them; status in the system's vocabulary (applied, going for an interview, interviewed, didn't get the job); a note of one line with dates. Confidential clients are described by sector and route ("via recruiter"). Nothing goes in as applied until it has actually gone in; built documents waiting on the person are not applications.

## Absences and rearrangements

State the request in the first line. Declare dates and duration. State that work search continues and how (remote interviews, applications, correspondence). Cite the rule that permits the absence, and cite the right rule for the right benefit; regimes differ and a wrong citation to a decision-maker is worse than none. Offer alternatives with dates. Keep case law out of the note; it is for a dispute afterwards, not a request beforehand.

## Discipline

Every claim in the journal matches the record elsewhere. Dates are exact. Numbers of applications and interviews are counts the person could produce evidence for. The tone is compliance, not grievance, even when the process deserves grievance.
