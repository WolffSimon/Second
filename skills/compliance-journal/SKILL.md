# Compliance journal

Some job seekers must keep a record of their search for a benefits system or an outplacement provider. Others keep one because it shows them what they have done. Either way, the journal is written to be checkable, dated and plain, because it may be read by someone who decides on payments.

## The long-form period update

One entry per period, headed with its dates and a line linking it to the previous entry. `references/period_update_template.md` gives the layout. Sections, in this order: outcomes received (each with the date and the stated reason, closed properly); interviews and recruiter calls (dated, with the role and the company or "confidential mandate via recruiter" where the client is confidential); applications made (a numbered list, company and role, date, one-line status); materials produced (a count of tailored documents); recruiter and search-firm engagement (names of firms, not of confidential clients); training and qualifications (passed, booked, in progress); profile, networking and speaking (posts, events, press); planned next steps. The planned steps in one entry are the first lines checked in the next.

## The job-entry list

Each entry: role title and company as the journal system wants them; status in the system's own vocabulary (for example: applied, interviewing, offered, unsuccessful); a note of one line with dates. Confidential clients are described by sector and route ("via recruiter"). Nothing goes in as applied until it has actually gone in; built documents waiting on the person are not applications.

## Absences and rearrangements

State the request in the first line. Declare dates and duration. If your work search continues during the absence, say so and how (remote interviews, applications, correspondence). If the system has a published rule that permits the absence, quote it from the official source and give the link. Never cite a rule from memory: rules differ between systems and change over time, and a wrong citation to a decision-maker is worse than none. If unsure, say so and ask the person to check with their adviser. Offer alternatives with dates.

## Discipline

Every claim in the journal matches the record elsewhere. Dates are exact. Numbers of applications and interviews are counts the person could produce evidence for. The tone is factual throughout.
