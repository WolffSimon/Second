# Recruiter notes

## Register

Normal business English, direct, warm enough to be human, in the person's voice. Subject, verb, object. One claim per sentence. 120 to 200 words for a first note; 60 to 100 for a follow-up. Short paragraphs, readable on a phone, because that is where a recruiter reads it.

## The licensed opener

A recruiter note may open "I am a twenty-year operations and technology executive" or its equivalent. This is the one genre where the identity opener is permitted, because the reader expects it and is skimming for altitude. It is followed immediately by receipts.

## What goes in

- Interest stated in the first line, with the role named as they named it.
- Two to four receipts, each with a number or a name, chosen for this reader's brief.
- The adjacent record stated affirmatively where the fit is imperfect. Not the gap.
- Logistics the reader needs: availability, location as a place name, contract vehicle if relevant, any fixed dates.
- "CV attached" or "CV on request", and an offer to talk this week.
- Name and number.

## What never goes in

Volunteered defects. Journey times. Salary before they ask, unless a form demands it, in which case one number and one line of reasoning. Flattery of the company. Anything from the job description in its own words. Em dashes.

## Variants

- **Direct message on a platform:** shorter, no subject line, contact details at the end.
- **Reply to a rejection:** thank, accept, one line of feedback if it is true and useful, keep the door open for other mandates in one sentence. Then stop.
- **Rearranging a meeting:** state the request in the first line, give the reason in one sentence, offer alternatives with dates, and attach any compliance you must show.
- **Speculative note to a referrer's contact:** three paragraphs, the connection named, the record in two sentences, an offer of a conversation before anything formal.

## Before sending

Read it aloud as the person. Check every sentence has a subject and a verb. Check no sentence would need a second sentence to explain it. Check the number and the name at the end.
