# Recruiter notes

## Register

Ordinary, conservative business English, direct, warm enough to be human, in the person's voice. Begin "Dear [name]". Say what the note is for in the first line. Subject, verb, object, with medium-length sentences joined by "which" and "and" where two facts belong together. 120 to 200 words for a first note; 60 to 100 for a follow-up. Short paragraphs, readable on a phone, because that is where a recruiter reads it.

## The licensed opener

A recruiter note may open "I am a [twenty]-year [field] executive" or its equivalent. Notes and letters are one of the two places `prose-law` permits the identity opener, because the reader expects it and is skimming for seniority. It is followed immediately by receipts.

## What goes in

- Interest stated in the first line, with the role named as they named it.
- Two to four receipts, each with a number or a name, chosen for this reader's brief.
- The nearest true record, stated plainly, where the fit is imperfect. Never the gap.
- Logistics the reader needs: availability, location as a place name, how you would be engaged if it matters (employed, or contracting through your own company), any fixed dates.
- "CV attached" or "CV on request", and an offer to talk this week.
- Name and number.

## What never goes in

Volunteered defects or anything the person has not done. Journey times. Salary before they ask, unless the posting asks for it, in which case one number and one line of reasoning. Flattery of the company. Anything from the job description in its own words. Words from this method's workshop ("receipt", "inventory"). Em dashes.

## Variants

- **Direct message on a platform:** shorter, no subject line, contact details at the end.
- **Reply to a rejection:** thank, accept, one line of feedback if it is true and useful, keep the door open for other mandates in one sentence. Then stop.
- **Rearranging a meeting:** state the request in the first line, give the reason in one sentence, offer alternatives with dates, and attach any document the other side has asked for.
- **Speculative note to a referrer's contact:** three paragraphs, the connection named, the record in two sentences, an offer of a conversation before anything formal.

## Before sending

Read it aloud as the person. Check every sentence has a subject and a verb. Check no sentence would need a second sentence to explain it. Check the number and the name at the end.
