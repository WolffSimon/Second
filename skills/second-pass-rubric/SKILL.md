# Second pass rubric

A first build is a draft until this has run. It is the builder's own check. The cold review rounds in `cold-review-loop` come after it, because a builder reading its own work sees its intentions, not the page.

## (a) Identify what the document does not answer

Take the posting line by line. For each requirement, find the sentence in the document that answers it with an instance: a thing done, with a number or a name. A sentence that answers with an adjective, a habit-claim or the posting's own words does not count. List the unanswered lines.

## (b) Search the inventory

For each unanswered line, search the whole inventory, not the part that seems relevant. Receipts sit under the era they happened in, and the relevant one is often filed under a different function. Place every receipt found in the role entry where it happened, at the level of the seat. Re-check numbers once after placing.

## (c) Ask for what remains

Run `excavation-interview` on the lines still unanswered. Apply the answers. Bank them with `receipts-inventory`. A "no" closes the line: the document stays silent there, under "The confession problem" in `prose-law`.

## The embarrassment test

Read every sentence as the person, across a table from the reader, and ask: would I be embarrassed to defend this? It catches what the other passes miss: a day's work reported as an achievement; the reader's own vocabulary borrowed for the person's organisation; a heroic verb where a plain one is true; a volunteer credential standing in a profile; a number told twice for emphasis; a claim the profile makes larger than the body supports; the device from a banned family that returned under momentum.

## Profile against entries

Every receipt in the profile must have a home in the role entry for the job where it happened, in different words. The profile summarises; the entries are the record a reader checks it against. A long profile above thin entries reads as talk. When the profile grows, move receipts down into the entries, and keep the profile to two paragraphs unless the reader already knows the person (a referral or a warm introduction).

## The normative test

Ask what a normal CV for this seat looks like to a reader who knows nothing about the person, and whether this one departs from it in a way that provokes a question before the receipts are read. Ordering by weight never overrides the reader's expectation of recency and scale: the most recent senior paid role opens unless the departure is one the reader will accept on sight. A voluntary position, an early role or a credential does not open a profile.

## Language pass

Every paragraph opener checked for the fronted noun phrase and the identity announcement. Every sentence checked for absolutes, balanced antitheses and metaphor where a literal phrase exists. Every compound sentence with more than one subject split. Every echo of the posting rewritten.

## Numbers once, again

Placing receipts moves numbers. Re-sweep the document for any figure appearing twice without a reason, and for any figure in a keyword block.

## Report

Tell the person what was placed, from where, and what remains as questions. Then hand the document to `cold-review-loop`.
