# Voice note to document

The expensive part of any document is the thinking, and thinking happens at walking pace. The recorder stops it evaporating. This skill turns the recording into paper.

## Before the walk: the question set

Write the questions the document will need, grouped by the section each feeds and numbered so the speaker can say "seven" and move on. Order them so the problem is described before the product, the facts before the opinions. Two sentences per question. Say what "enough" looks like for an answer. End with two or three questions for the record: what is confidential, who approves, what would make the whole thing wrong.

## Decoding the transcript

Transcripts of walks contain traffic, birds, greetings to strangers, wrong turns, food and the occasional message to someone else. Read the whole thing before extracting anything. Then:

- Match each answer to its question by number or by content. Speakers answer out of order and skip.
- Treat mis-transcriptions as puzzles, not facts: a brand name mangled by the transcriber is checked against what the speaker could plausibly have said. Never let a mis-hearing into a document.
- Separate facts, opinions and instructions. Facts are banked; opinions are labelled as the person's view when used; instructions ("look this up", "don't mention that", "use the public name for it") are obeyed, within the rule that no fact enters a document unless it is in the inventory.
- Note explicit refusals ("I don't want to answer that") and never fill them.
- Note the personal asides separately. They are not noise; they are the person's life, and a personal journal, if the person keeps one, may want them. They do not enter the document.

## Building the document

Build from the answers in the person's register, per `prose-law`. Where an answer is an opinion from experience, label it as such in the document. Where an answer says "look it up," research it and cite the source rather than the memory. Where an answer names something confidential, write the generic form. Version the document and state in its header what each version added.

## Research passes

After the first build, run the questions the person flagged for research. Add findings as sections with their sources. Rewrite any section whose economics or facts the research changed, and say so in the version note.

## After the document

Report what changed, what the transcript corrected in the record, and what the next round of questions is. Bank every new fact the same day. File the personal asides wherever the person keeps them.
